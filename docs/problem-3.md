## Question 1: What is something you believe strongly that most people around you disagree with?

### Proposed Response Draft:
I believe strongly that **unlimited choice and over-customization in software design are counterproductive, and that opinionated systems with strict constraints create better user experiences.** 

Most product teams and developers believe that giving users more settings, more toggle buttons, and more customization options is a path to a better product. They assume that user satisfaction is directly proportional to control. However, I believe this is a design cop-out. When we make every layout, feature, and workflow customizable, we are offloading the hard work of making design decisions onto the user. 

Constraints reduce cognitive load. By building opinionated software that enforces a specific, optimized workflow (like Apple's styling or Basecamp's simple project organization), we free users to focus on their actual work rather than configuring their tools. In design, less choice often leads to higher focus and velocity.

## Question 2: Name one thing in your city or daily life that you think is fundamentally broken. How would you redesign it from scratch?

### Proposed Response Draft:
The thing in my daily life that I believe is fundamentally broken is **the appointment and queue scheduling system at public health centers and local administration offices.** 

Currently, the experience involves taking physical paper tokens, standing in crowded corridors for hours with no status updates, and experiencing high anxiety because there is no visibility into how many people are ahead or how fast the queue is moving.

#### The Redesign: A Decentralized "Time-Rationed" Digital Roster
If I were to redesign this from scratch, I would build a system based on three core pillars:

1.  **Virtual SMS/App Queues with Dynamic Travel-Time Padding**: Instead of booking a rigid time slot (e.g., 2:30 PM), users receive a position-based queue token. The system tracks the physical location of the patient and average transit times in the city. It sends a "leave now" push notification or SMS alert only when they are 3 positions away from the counter, based on real-time transit times.
2.  **Anonymous Live Progress Dashboard**: Large screens in the clinic and a simple web link show the anonymized ticket numbers currently being served, average processing time per person, and standard deviation. This sets realistic expectations and reduces waiting anxiety.
3.  **Predictive Load Balancing**: The system analyzes historical traffic data to offer "incentive tokens" (e.g., priority next-visit booking or fee waivers) to patients who schedule visits during historical low-activity windows, naturally leveling the occupancy curve of the clinic.
